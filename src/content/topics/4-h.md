## Begin with the club's approved project

A 4-H club fundraiser can support project materials, educational travel, shared equipment, or a community service activity. Start by defining the purpose, the amount needed, and the organization responsible for receiving the proceeds. Distinguish a club project from an individual member's expenses so supporters understand exactly what their purchase or donation supports.

Before using the club name, emblem, or an online campaign account, ask the appropriate local program contact which approvals and financial procedures apply. This guide is planning information, not permission to run a campaign. Provider availability does not establish authorization from a club, county program, or other governing organization.

## Select work that supports youth learning

A fundraiser can give members practical experience in planning, communication, record keeping, and evaluating a project. Choose age-appropriate responsibilities and keep adults accountable for contracts, payments, and public accounts. Members can help compare options, write approved invitations, organize materials, and thank supporters without handling every financial task.

A preorder sale can give the club a defined order window. An online product store may connect relatives outside the local area. Merchandise can mark a shared project or event when approved artwork is available. Match the choice to the club's meeting schedule and volunteer support, rather than adding a complex delivery obligation during a busy project season.

## Calculate what remains for the project

Product revenue is not the same as club proceeds. Compare the retained amount after product costs, shipping, payment charges, and any club-paid supplies. Find out whether orders must reach a minimum and whether the club is responsible for unsold goods. A no-inventory format can reduce some responsibilities without eliminating communication and reconciliation work.

As a hypothetical example, a $600 materials budget would require 60 completed orders producing $10 each in net proceeds before other expenses. This is not a typical margin or a sales forecast. Use the calculation to decide whether the project fits the club's size and whether a smaller goal would reduce pressure on members and households.

## Keep the fundraiser inclusive and supervised

Members may live far apart, have different internet access, or share the same pool of local supporters. Provide several useful ways to contribute rather than measuring participation only through sales. Keep personal details out of public campaign materials unless the responsible adults have approved their use under the program's procedures.

A local sale can teach customer service but needs safe, supervised arrangements for contact and delivery. Online sales can reduce group distribution work while still requiring families to share a clear message. Set a realistic campaign period and avoid repeated appeals to the same households. Agree in advance on who answers questions and what happens if an order is delayed or incorrect.

## Compare provider formats after approval

Fundraising.com offers multiple product-sale formats, Giving Bean provides coffee and tea programs, Bonfire supports custom merchandise, and Double Good offers online popcorn stores. These are general providers to research, not official 4-H partners or endorsed programs. Review the profiles below and confirm both provider eligibility and the club's own approval requirements before proceeding.

After the campaign, invite members to compare the original budget with the actual net result and volunteer time. Report how the money will support the approved project and keep the records with the club's responsible adult officers. A short review turns the fundraiser into a useful learning experience and gives future club leaders evidence for their next decision.
