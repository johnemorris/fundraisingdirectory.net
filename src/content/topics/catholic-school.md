## Separate school needs from parish appeals

A Catholic school campaign may share supporters with a parish, religious education program, and other ministries. Begin by naming the school need and identifying the organization that will receive the funds. Tuition assistance, classroom technology, and a building project need different explanations and spending plans. Families should understand whether their support goes to the school, a parent association, or a broader parish account.

Discuss the campaign calendar with school and parish leaders before approaching households. Avoid putting a new product sale beside a major parish appeal simply because both committees have access to the same mailing list. Agree on one person who can resolve scheduling conflicts.

## Choose between ongoing and seasonal fundraising

An ongoing purchasing program may fit families who want to support the school through routine spending. It needs a coordinator who can explain enrollment, answer account questions, and reconcile earnings over time. It is less suitable for an urgent bill unless the school understands when earnings become available and how participation will develop.

A seasonal catalog, coffee sale, or school activity campaign provides a clearer beginning and end. Match the format to volunteer capacity. A direct-ship program can reduce distribution work, while a local preorder sale may benefit from an established pickup routine after school or a community gathering. Confirm permission before using either location.

## Evaluate proceeds without relying on a headline rate

Compare the amount available for the school after product costs, payment charges, shipping, and organizer-paid supplies. Ongoing reward programs can have different rates by purchase type or merchant. Seasonal sales can have order thresholds or delivery conditions. Ask for terms that apply to the actual program your committee plans to use.

For a hypothetical goal of $2,400, 160 participating households would need to generate an average of $15 each in net school proceeds. That calculation does not predict household spending or provider performance. Use it to test whether the target is plausible and whether a smaller project or longer timeline would be more responsible.

## Keep participation welcoming and accounts clear

A school community includes households with different incomes, schedules, languages, and connections to the parish. Provide a simple explanation of the fundraiser and avoid making purchasing feel like a condition of belonging. Give volunteers roles beyond sales, such as translation, order checking, setup, or thank-you messages.

Decide how money will be recorded and reported before launch. If the school uses family-specific fundraising credits, ask its leadership to confirm the applicable policy before describing any individual benefit. Do not promise tax treatment, tuition offsets, or a personal entitlement to campaign proceeds. Keep the published invitation focused on the school-approved purpose and direct policy questions to the responsible administrator.
